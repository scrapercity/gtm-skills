# Signal Plays

What to send once a signal is scored: who gets it, the touch plan, and a complete first email for each signal that starts outside ScraperCity (S14 to S28). Detection, half-lives and touch windows are in `references/signal-catalog.md`; tiers and SLAs in `references/scoring-and-freshness.md`.

Every email below is an illustrative example for a list-building offer: company names are fictional, and every proof line (a client result with a number) must be replaced with the sender's own measured result before it goes out. Bodies stay at or under 90 words. In a program, write each one as a sequencer template: the event fact and the proof result become merge fields (`{{open_roles}}`, `{{round}}`, `{{proof_result}}`), with a "Fields:" line naming each field's source column and an example value.

Spacing for every touch plan below comes from Instantly's 2026 benchmark guide (March 2026): 4 to 7 emails over 14 to 21 days, the first follow-up 2 to 3 days after the first email, later ones 4 to 7 days apart; 58% of replies arrive on step 1. Calls and LinkedIn requests sit between the emails.

Rules that hold for every play:

| Rule | Setting |
|-|-|
| First line | Names the dated event in plain words (Berman: a dated reason in the first line, connected to the offer), or for S15 and S22 a fit fact |
| Ask | One question per email (Berman's short ask) |
| Claims | A claim about the prospect's world is asked as a question or comes from the sender's own data; product capabilities the user has not stated go in one "Claims to confirm" line |
| Stop | Stop the sequence on any reply; a "remove me" goes on the one suppression list the same day |
| Threads | A few people per account per signal, each with the angle for their own function; never the same body to two people at one account |
| Cool-down | One signal sequence per account per 90 days; Instantly advises pausing two to three months before re-engaging (`references/scoring-and-freshness.md` section 6) |

## 1. Who gets each signal

| Signal | First contact | Second thread (after touch 2 with no reply) |
|-|-|-|
| S14 identified visit | The identified person if the title fits; a visitor below the buyer (rep, SDR, coordinator): the buyer at that domain, with the fit-based email and no mention of the visit | The buyer's manager |
| S14 second visitor at an account in sequence | No new sequence; the visit adds to the score | The second thread, with that person's function angle |
| S15 company visit | The buyer title at the domain | None until a second signal fires |
| S16 champion moved | The champion, from the rep who knew them | None; one person, one relationship |
| S17 funding, S22 Form D | CEO under 50 employees; else the leader of the function the money is for | The person who will run the build (head of sales ops, engineering manager) |
| S18 job post | Hiring manager or CEO while the post is open | The new hire, as S20, once hired |
| S20 new leader | The new leader | Their direct report who runs the work day to day |
| S21 champion left a customer | The replacement, from the account owner | The economic buyer above them |
| S23 earnings or filing | The executive who owns the named initiative | A director in that function, with the operational version |
| S24 competitor change | The owner of the workflow the change hits | The admin of the competitor tool |
| S25 account launch | The launch owner | The person running the new team or market |
| S26 event | The person at the booth, on stage or registered | A colleague listed for the same event |
| S19 social | The person who posted or commented | None |
| S27 G2 intent | The buyer title at the domain | The day-to-day owner of the problem |
| S28 webinar or content | The person who engaged if the title fits; else the buyer at the domain | The buyer, once the first contact replies or goes quiet |

## 2. Website visitors (S14, S15)

Touch plan, S14 inside the program's touch window:

| Step | Channel | Content |
|-|-|-|
| 1 | Call if a mobile is on file | Fit-based opener; ask who owns the problem |
| 1 | Email 1 | Below |
| Between emails | LinkedIn connection request, no note | None |
| 2 | Email 2, 2 to 3 days later | One client result in their segment |
| Between emails | Call | Reference email 2 |
| 3 | Email 3, 4 to 7 days later | One question; close the thread |

S15 rows go to the tier B campaign with the same fit-based copy as any account in the segment.

> Subject: Denver routing at Ledgerline
>
> Hi Maya, saw Ledgerline opened a Denver office in August.
>
> Did the round-robin rules for the new territory get rebuilt by hand, or did they carry over?
>
> We set up lead routing for B2B finance teams in a day. One client cut speed-to-lead from 4 hours to 11 minutes.
>
> Who owns routing for the Denver team?

## 3. Funding and Form D (S17, S22)

Use the round's stated purpose when the announcement gives one. Map it to the persona and angle:

| Stated use of the money | Persona | Angle |
|-|-|-|
| Grow the sales team or go to market | CRO, VP Sales, sales ops | Ramp time and pipeline per new rep |
| Build product or hire engineers | CTO, VP Engineering | Hiring pipeline, tooling for a larger team |
| Enter new markets or countries | Head of growth, country lead | Local lists, local channels, launch timing |
| Scale customer success | VP Customer Success | Onboarding volume, retention at the new size |
| No stated use | CEO at a small company | One question about the next two quarters |

Touch plan: email to the first contact, LinkedIn request, an email with a result from a company at the same stage, an email to the second thread with their function's angle, a last email, on Instantly's spacing. An S18 post or S20 hire on the team you serve adds to the score.

S17, announced round:

> Subject: the sales hiring plan at Halden
>
> Hi Sam, saw Halden's Series A news on Tuesday and the line about doubling the sales team by mid-2027.
>
> Every new rep needs a territory list before the first send.
>
> We hand each new SDR a validated list for their territory before day one. One client cut ramp from 7 weeks to 3.
>
> Is list building already covered in the onboarding plan?

S22, Form D with no press. The filing is not named:

> Subject: sales headcount at Brightline
>
> Hi Ana, are you adding sales headcount at Brightline this half, or building pipeline for the team you have first?
>
> We supply validated lead lists by territory, so new reps start with accounts on day one.
>
> One seed-stage client went from 0 to 22 meetings a month in their first quarter of outbound.
>
> Worth sending a sample of 50 contacts from your top segment?

## 4. Hiring and new leaders (S18, S20)

Two tracks on one account: the hiring manager while the post is open, then the new leader once the S20 re-pull or an announcement shows them in the seat.

| When | Action |
|-|-|
| Post first seen | Email the hiring manager or CEO (below) |
| Next follow-up | One result from a team that hired the same role |
| While the post is open | Re-check the post each pull; when it closes, run the S20 re-pull for that domain |
| New leader in the seat | Email the new leader (S20, below) |
| Inside their first 90 days | Further touches tied to their first-quarter plan (UserGems: new executives re-evaluate the stack in their first 90 days, vendor claim) |
| After day 90 | Move to the static campaign for the segment |

S18:

> Subject: the two SDR seats in Austin
>
> Hi Priya, saw Corvane is hiring two SDRs in Austin, and the posting says they will own outbound to mid-market logistics.
>
> Before they send a single email, someone has to build and clean those lists.
>
> We deliver a validated, segmented list per rep before their start date.
>
> Who is building their lists today?

S20:

> Subject: first 90 days at Corvane
>
> Hi Jordan, saw you joined Corvane as VP Revenue in September.
>
> Is the outbound stack part of your first-quarter review?
>
> If list quality is on it, we can pull 100 validated contacts for your top segment this week at no cost.

## 5. Job changes (S16, S21)

Both sides of one job change. The new company gets S16 from the rep who knew the person; the old company, if it is a customer, gets S21 from its account owner.

| Seniority of the person who moved | S16 channel | S21 urgency at the old customer |
|-|-|-|
| VP and above | Personal email from the rep who worked with them, then a call | Account owner writes to the replacement; escalate when renewal is near |
| Manager | Personal email, then the S16 campaign | Account owner writes to the replacement |
| Individual contributor | S16 campaign | Note on the account; replacement found at the next quarterly re-pull |

S16, sent inside the new hire's first 90 days:

> Subject: Fernway's email program
>
> Hi Dana, congrats on the new role at Fernway.
>
> At Northpaw you took repeat orders from 18% to 26% of revenue with the flows we built together.
>
> Fernway sells to a similar buyer, so the same three flows likely apply.
>
> Are you rebuilding the email program there, or inheriting one that works?

S21, from the account owner to the replacement:

> Subject: handover for the Halden account
>
> Hi Lee, Morgan ran our list program at Halden until last month, and I understand the outbound budget sits with you now.
>
> Where it stands: 4 campaigns live, 38 meetings booked since March, renewal on January 15.
>
> Can I take 20 minutes next week to walk you through what is running and what Morgan planned next?

## 6. Earnings and filings (S23)

Quarterly loop per watched public company:

| When | Action |
|-|-|
| Filing day | Pull the 8-K with Item 2.02, the 10-Q or 10-K, and the call transcript; write an initiative card: quote, section, owner, your question |
| First email | Email the initiative owner (below) |
| Follow-up | A question built from one Item 1A risk factor, in the filing's own words |
| Second thread | Director in the same function: the operational version of the same point |
| Next filing | Refresh the card; drop quotes that management stopped repeating |

Follow-up built from a risk factor, in the filing's words:

> Subject: the onboarding risk in your 10-K
>
> Hi Rafael, Item 1A of the 10-K lists slower onboarding of mid-market customers as a risk to revenue growth.
>
> Is that showing up yet as longer time to first value, or is it still a forecast?

Write several cards per account before sending. The quote must come from the filing or the call, never from a news summary, and carry the section (Item 7, Item 1A, or the call's Q&A).

> Subject: cost to serve in mid-market
>
> Hi Rafael, your Q3 MD&A names lowering cost to serve in the mid-market segment as next year's priority.
>
> We cut onboarding tickets 30% for a payments company at your scale by moving account setup into the product.
>
> Is self-serve onboarding part of that plan, or is it mostly headcount?

## 7. Competitor changes (S24)

Two motions the same day: account owners brief champions in open deals, and the install base from Tech Stack Search gets a campaign.

| Who | When | What |
|-|-|-|
| Champion in an open deal with that competitor | Same day | Three lines: what changed, what it means for their project, one question |
| Install-base accounts that pass fit and exclusions | Inside the program's touch window | Email 1 below, then follow-ups on Instantly's spacing |
| Product marketing | After the change | Updated comparison talk track for reps |

> Subject: API on the Enterprise tier
>
> Hi Chris, saw Relaywise moved its API to the Enterprise tier on October 1.
>
> Teams on the Growth plan that sync to a CRM now face an upgrade or a rebuild before renewal.
>
> We migrate that sync in a week, and our API is on every plan.
>
> Is the integration work on your list this quarter?

## 8. Account launches (S25)

Touch plan: email to the launch owner, a follow-up with one client result from a launch, a last email to the person running the new team, on Instantly's spacing.

> Subject: onboarding for the payroll add-on
>
> Hi Mei, saw Brightline launched the payroll add-on on October 6.
>
> We write and run onboarding sequences for B2B launches; one HR software client took activation from 41% to 63% in 60 days.
>
> Who is building the onboarding emails for the add-on?

## 9. Events (S26)

Booth staff are selling to their own prospects, and shows such as NAMM bar non-exhibitors from soliciting exhibitors in aisles or booths (NAMM Show suitcasing policy, checked 2026-10-13). So an exhibitor whose sales team is your buyer gets a pre-event email to the sales leader who is not working the booth, asking for a meeting in the week after the show; a booth visit is an optional drop-by. Attendees and speakers can be asked for a named time and place at the show.

| When | Action |
|-|-|
| Lists published | Copy exhibitor, sponsor and speaker lists; map to domains; pull the sales leader and booth team with the Lead Database |
| Pre-event start date set in the program | Exhibitors: email the sales leader for a post-show meeting. Attendees and speakers: a named time and place at the show |
| Follow-up on Instantly's spacing | Follow-up; LinkedIn request |
| Event days | Log every conversation the same day: name, company, topic, next step |
| After the event | Post-event email to everyone you met first, then the booked post-show meetings, then a theme email to exhibitors you did not meet |
| Max age set in the program | Drop the row; move to the static campaign |

Pre-event, exhibitor, to the sales leader:

> Subject: after RetailOps Summit
>
> Hi Dana, saw Kestrel Coffee is exhibiting at RetailOps Summit in Denver, booth 412.
>
> Will the booth leads include wholesale buyers who need a reorder email before their second order? We build those for roasters.
>
> Could we take 20 minutes the week after the show to look at how the booth leads convert? I will also be on the floor if you want to drop by.

Post-event, met at the booth:

> Subject: the reorder question from Tuesday
>
> Hi Tom, good to meet you at booth 412 on Tuesday.
>
> You asked how other roasters time reorder emails for wholesale accounts. Ours go out 5 days before each account's usual reorder date, based on its last three orders.
>
> I put the flow in a one-page doc. Want me to send it?

## 10. Social engagement (S19)

| Engagement | First action | Then |
|-|-|-|

| Comment on a post about the problem | Email naming the problem in their words | Call for tier A |

## 11. G2 intent and webinars (S27, S28)

S27 names no person and the buyer did not ask to be contacted, so the email never mentions G2 or the research; it uses a fit fact or another live signal, and the intent raises the tier. Compare and competitive signals go to the competitor angle from S24 when the compared product is known.

S28, attended a webinar:

> Subject: the list-decay question from Thursday
>
> Hi Ava, thanks for joining Thursday's session on list decay.
>
> You stayed for the part on re-validating lists before each send. We run that check on a sample of your current list at no cost and show what share would bounce.
>
> Want me to run it on 500 contacts from your main segment?

Registered and did not attend: the recording plus one question about the topic, same day as the session.

## 12. ScraperCity signals (S1 to S13)

First lines for these are in `references/signal-catalog.md` under each signal, and full emails built on the same fields are in `references/cold-email-frameworks.md` (trigger-based relevance, offer-led and problem-first sections). Touch plans follow the tier table: tier A gets a call and email the same day, tier B the signal campaign, both on Instantly's spacing with the signal named in touch 1.

## 13. Measure each play

Log `signal_id`, age at first touch, persona and touch number on every send. Once a signal reaches the contact count the program sets, compare positive reply rate and meetings per 100 contacts with a static list from the same ICP and the same weeks. Retire a play, or rewrite its angle, when it falls short of the static list; give a play that beats it more accounts. Without your own static baseline yet, compare against published averages: Hunter reports 3% for sales outreach sequences (4.5% across all uses, 31 million emails sent in 2025) and Instantly 3.43% across all sequences (2026 benchmark).
