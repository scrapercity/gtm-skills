# Subject Line Library

Original subject lines grouped by angle. All are under 50 characters before merge values and avoid emoji, all caps and fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" prefixes. The subject must match the first line of the email: if the subject mentions reviews, the first line is about reviews.

Background from Alex Berman's public work: his 2017 Entrepreneur article lists four types (referral, congratulations on news, generic but customized, fully generic) and says relevance drives opens; his Single Grain video favors short neutral lines such as "Quick question" or "Question about" plus the company name, and warns that a pitch in the subject gets the email deleted unread; his templates page calls "Quick Question" its control.

Because Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates opens, judge subject tests on replies, and only after the offer and segment tests are settled.

## 10. Subject line rules

| Rule | Why |
|-|-|
| Two to six words, under 50 characters | Fits a phone preview; Berman's templates page sets the 50 character cap |
| Lowercase or sentence case | Reads like a person, not a newsletter |
| No pitch, price, "free", or percentage | Pitch subjects get deleted unread (Berman, Single Grain) |
| Skip emoji, all caps and exclamation marks | Promotional signals |
| Never "Re:" or "Fwd:" on a first touch | Misleading under CAN-SPAM, and readers notice |
| Fallback for every merge tag | "Question about " with an empty name looks broken |
| Matches the first line | A news subject with no news in the body reads as bait (Berman, Entrepreneur) |
