# Response Library

Original responses for each reply class. Every response is under 80 words, has one ask, and offers specific times or a booking link when the person is interested. Brackets are edited per reply; fill them with real details only. Sign with the sender's first name.

Principles from Alex Berman's follow-up guide and templates page: answer interest fast, keep "send info" replies to a short summary with one case study and a proposed call, do not send pricing or PDFs, acknowledge and remove "not interested", log "later" dates, and ask competitor users what made them choose that vendor.

## 1. Interested: meeting

**R1. Two slots**

> Great, thanks [first name]. Does Tuesday at 10:30am or Wednesday at 2pm Central work for 15 minutes? I'll send an invite to this address with a dial-in.

**R2. Booking link**

> Thanks [first name]. Here's my calendar: [link]. If none of those times fit, send two that do and I'll make one work.

**R3. They proposed a time**

> Thursday at 11 works. Invite sent to [email]. Anyone else from [company] I should add?

**R4. Interested, but busy this week**

> No rush. Would Monday the [date] at 10am or Tuesday the [date] at 3pm suit you better?

## 2. Interested: info first

**R5. "Send more info"** (Berman: short summary, one case study, a proposed call, no PDF)

> Short version: we [mechanism] for [niche]. A [client type] in [city] went from [before] to [after] in [time]. The details depend on your [setup], so a 15 minute call is faster than a deck. Tuesday at 10am or Wednesday at 2pm?

**R6. "How much does it cost?"**

> It depends on [number of locations / volume]. Most [client type] land between [low] and [high] a month. Can we take 10 minutes Thursday so I can give you an exact number for [company]?

(Give a real range only if your pricing allows it; otherwise drop the second sentence.)

**R7. "Is there a case study?"**

> Yes. [Client type] in [city]: [before] to [after] in [time], mostly from [mechanism]. Happy to walk through their numbers on a quick call. Does Wednesday work?

## 3. Interested: product question

**R8. Integration or fit question**

> Yes, it works with [tool]. Setup takes about [time] and your team doesn't change anything in [tool]. Want me to show you on a 15 minute call?

**R9. Question we can't answer with a yes**

> Not today. We [alternative]. If that still works for [company], I can show you how in 15 minutes. Worth a look?

## 4. Referral

**R10. Thank the referrer (named person)**

> Thanks [first name], I'll reach out to [referral first name] and mention you sent me. Appreciate it.

(No question needed. This reply closes the thread with the referrer.)

**R11. First email to the referred person** (send from the referral sequence)

> Hi [referral first name], [referrer first name] suggested I reach out about [problem]. We helped a [client type] go from [before] to [after] in [time]. Worth 15 minutes to see if it fits [company]?

**R12. Role only ("our marketing team handles that")**

> Thanks [first name]. Who on the marketing team should I talk to?

## 5. Objections

**R13. No need**

> Fair enough, thanks for saying so. Out of curiosity, is that because [problem] is already handled, or because it isn't a priority this year?

(One question only. If they answer with no, close the thread with R25.)

**R14. Already use a competitor** (Berman: ask why, position as complementary)

> Makes sense, [competitor] is solid. What made you pick them? Several of our clients run us alongside [competitor] for [specific gap], so it may not be either-or.

**R15. Price or no budget**

> Understood. Would a [pilot / one location / one month] at [smaller scope] make sense to test it first, or is this a next-year conversation?

**R16. "We tried something like this and it didn't work"**

> That's useful to know. What went wrong last time? If it was [common failure], that's the part we handle differently, and I can show you how in 15 minutes.

**R17. "Just send me something to read"**

> Will do. Here's a one-line summary: [result for client type]. If it's relevant after you read it, should I hold Thursday at 10am for a short call?

**R18. "Is this automated?" or "Is this AI?"**

> I write these myself, using public business data to pick who to contact. Happy to answer anything about how we found you. Is [problem] something you're working on?

(Answer honestly. If a tool drafted the email, say a person reviewed it.)

## 6. Not now and timing

**R19. Specific future date**

> Thanks, I'll follow up the week of [date]. Is this still the best address?

**R20. Vague "not now"**

> No problem. When would be a better time to check back, next quarter or next year?

**R21. Re-engagement on the scheduled date** (new email, same thread)

> Hi [first name], you asked me to check back around now about [problem]. Is this a better time, or should I try again in [month]?

## 7. Wrong person and left company

**R22. Wrong person**

> Thanks [first name], sorry for the misfire. Who handles [problem area] at [company]?

**R23. Left the company (human reply)**

> Thanks for letting me know, and good luck with what's next. Who took over [role] at [company]?

## 8. Unsubscribe and angry

**R24. Unsubscribe**

No reply. Suppress the address in every tool within 1 business day. If your process requires a confirmation, send at most:

> Done, you won't hear from me again.

**R25. Not interested, closing**

> Understood, thanks for letting me know. I won't follow up.

**R26. Angry or "how did you get my email"**

> Sorry for the unwanted email. I found your address in a business contact database, and I've removed it so you won't hear from us again.

Then suppress, and flag the reply for the weekly review. Never argue, never send a second message.

## 9. Automatic replies

No response to OOO, auto-acknowledgements, ticket systems or bounces. Pause or suppress per `references/reply-taxonomy-and-routing.md`.

## 10. Response rules

| Rule | Why |
|-|-|
| Under 80 words | The person already engaged; a long answer slows the next step |
| One ask | Two questions get one answered or neither |
| Two specific times in the prospect's time zone | Cuts the scheduling back-and-forth |
| No PDF, deck or price sheet as the first response | Berman's guidance: push for the call |
| Reply in the same thread | Keeps context for both sides |
| No sequence touches while a conversation is open | A scheduled follow-up landing mid-conversation looks automated |
| Always offer to remove on any hostile reply | Complaint prevention and CAN-SPAM opt-out duty |

## 11. Reply quality checklist

| Check | Pass |
|-|-|
| Class identified with priority order | Yes |
| Response under 80 words | Yes |
| Ends with one question (except R10, R24, R25, R26) | Yes |
| Times stated with a time zone | Yes |
| No attachment | Yes |
| CRM status changed | Yes |
| Suppression applied where required | Yes |
